# Candidate universe v1 — scoping the live pipeline's watchlist

*Written before the live pipeline ingests a single new filing under this scope. This is the same pre-registration discipline used for `PREREGISTRATION_v7.md`: the selection criteria are locked here, in writing, before any company is added or removed based on how interesting its events turn out to be.*

**Revision note:** the first draft of this document was reviewed by ChatGPT and independently re-verified against the actual code and the document's own table before any of the following corrections were accepted. All of them checked out: the table has 108 companies, not the 113 claimed in the prose; three names (TSM, NVO, MGA) are foreign private issuers that file on Form 6-K, which `edgar_ingest_worker.py`'s `TARGET_FORMS = {"8-K", "8-K/A"}` does not capture at all — they'd silently generate zero filings; the "relationship discovery happens organically" claim overstated what's built (see the corrected paragraph below); and the four delisted-company names flagged in the original draft don't actually appear anywhere in the table — a leftover from an earlier brainstorm list, not a live issue. This version fixes all of it.

## Why this document exists

`edgar_ingest_worker.py` polls a fixed watchlist of CIKs (see its docstring: "for a small watchlist of companies... polls SEC's public submissions API"). The fast prototype's watchlist was 8 tickers, all inside one AI-hardware supply chain, chosen because that scope was small enough to hand-verify with Yahoo Finance data. The v7/v8 rounds found exactly the limitation you'd expect from that choice: NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom all sit inside the same AI-capex macro regime, so even pooling three "different" catalysts didn't buy the statistical independence it looked like it should. The spec's own promotion gate (`diffusion-experiment-spec-v2.1.md`, Phase I-A → human-confirmed trades) requires "an effective, correlation-adjusted sample in the low hundreds... across ≥2 distinct volatility/rate regimes, no single trade/category driving the result."

**Correction after review — sector breadth and regime diversity are not the same thing.** The first draft of this document conflated them, claiming the narrow watchlist "cannot clear [the gate] no matter how long it runs, because it only ever samples one regime." That's wrong in an important way: "volatility/rate regime" in the spec is a *calendar-time* concept — a semiconductor-only universe run long enough would eventually see both rising- and falling-rate periods, both low- and high-VIX stretches. Its real problem is *category concentration* (one economic story driving every event), not a permanent inability to cross time regimes. Conversely, widening to 108 companies across 9 sectors and then running for one quarter still only samples whatever single regime that quarter happens to be in. **Sector breadth buys cross-sectional category diversity — it satisfies "no single trade/category driving the result." Elapsed time buys calendar-time regime diversity — it satisfies "≥2 distinct volatility/rate regimes."** The promotion gate needs both, and this document only addresses the first one; the second is a function of how long the pipeline runs, not how it's scoped today. It's also worth being honest that 9 sectors don't behave as 9 independent things even on the category-diversity axis: AI hardware, industrials, and parts of autos all share global-capex/manufacturing-cycle exposure; homebuilders, financials, and autos all carry rate/credit sensitivity even if the mechanism differs by sign; consumer retail and airlines/logistics share consumer-demand cyclicality. Energy and healthcare are the most genuinely orthogonal buckets here. So this scope is more like 4-6 meaningfully distinct exposure families than 9 independent ones — still a real improvement over one, but not a 9x one.

**Mechanical point, corrected after review:** widening the universe does *not* mean hand-curating more supplier/customer pairs the way the prototype did — `extraction_prompt_v1.md` already asks the LLM to extract `entity_a`, `entity_b`, `relationship_type`, and a `relationship_evidence` tier directly from whatever document it's given, and `schema.sql`'s `entity_relationships` table is built to store exactly that, generically. **That part of the first draft was correct.** What was overstated: the claim that relationship discovery would then "happen organically" as a consequence of widening the watchlist. It won't, yet, because it isn't wired up. A direct check of the actual codebase (not just the design docs) confirms `entity_relationships` and `candidate_signals` are only ever written to inside `tests/*.py` fixtures — no production code calls the extraction prompt against a real LLM, parses its JSON output, resolves the returned entity names (e.g. `"Ford Motor Company"`) to a stable `entity_id`, or writes the result into the database. `edgar_ingest_worker.py`'s own docstring says as much: extraction is explicitly "a separate extraction-prompt step," and that step's runner doesn't exist yet. So the honest version of this claim is: **the schema and prompt are correctly designed for generic, watchlist-agnostic relationship discovery, but the extraction runner → entity-resolution → relationship-writer → candidate-generation bridge is real, unbuilt engineering work** — arguably a bigger and more foundational task than finalizing this company list, and it's now the top item under "What's still not decided" below.

## Selection criteria (fixed before any company is scored on outcomes)

1. **Sector diversity over single-sector depth.** The whole point is regime independence, so the universe spans multiple economically distinct sectors rather than going deeper into AI hardware, even though that's the one sector already validated end-to-end.
2. **Active, liquid EDGAR filers.** Large- and mid-cap, actively traded, with a real history of 8-K filings — thinly-traded or rarely-reporting entities don't generate enough raw events to be useful and complicate market-data quality.
3. **Real supply-chain/competitive structure.** Companies plausible as *either* side of a named relationship disclosure — industrial and consumer-goods companies that actually name suppliers, customers, or partners in ordinary-course filings, not, e.g., pure holding companies or single-asset REITs with few relationship-bearing disclosures.
4. **No performance-based selection.** No company is added or excluded because of how its stock has moved historically, recently, or is expected to move. This list is locked by sector/structural criteria only, exactly the discipline `PREREGISTRATION_v7.md` used for catalyst dates.
5. **Corporate-action hygiene disclosed, not hidden.** A few names below have pending or recent M&A/delisting activity — flagged explicitly rather than silently dropped or silently kept, with a placeholder rule (see "Before this goes live" below).

## Proposed watchlist — 9 sectors, 108 companies

**Scope decision on foreign private issuers, made in this revision:** the first draft included TSM (Taiwan Semiconductor), NVO (Novo Nordisk), and MGA (Magna International) — all foreign private issuers that report current information on Form 6-K, not 8-K. `edgar_ingest_worker.py`'s `TARGET_FORMS = {"8-K", "8-K/A"}` doesn't capture 6-Ks at all, so as drafted those three would sit on the watchlist and silently contribute zero filings. Adding 6-K support is plausible later, but 6-K filing patterns vary a lot by issuer (TSM alone also files monthly revenue 6-Ks, which would flood extraction with mostly-irrelevant documents if included unfiltered) — that's a real design problem, not a config flag, and not worth solving before v1 launches. **Decision: exclude FPIs from v1 and replace all three with domestic 8-K filers in the same sector**, keeping the 6-K question as an explicit, separate future enhancement rather than a silent gap in a launched watchlist. TSM → Texas Instruments (TXN); NVO → AbbVie (ABBV); MGA → Dana Incorporated (DAN).

All 108 are domestic 8-K filers, so no filing-type gap against the current worker.

## Before this goes live — verification still required

This list was drafted from general knowledge, not verified against EDGAR the way every number in the v1–v8 prototype rounds was. Three things must happen before any CIK from this list is loaded into `edgar_ingest_worker.py`'s watchlist, matching this project's own standard of not trusting unverified claims:

1. **CIK lookup for all 108 tickers**, done directly against SEC's own company-search, not inferred from memory.
2. **A coverage sanity check per sector, with the threshold set before looking** — a lightweight pull of each company's filing history to confirm it actually generates a reasonable rate of 8-K/relationship-bearing disclosures, the same kind of empirical check `PROTOTYPE_README.md`'s "coverage is a measured empirical fact, not an asserted one" language (from `diffusion-experiment-spec-v2.2.1.md` §6) already commits this project to. The pass/fail threshold for "thin coverage, replace this name" must be fixed *before* running the check, not chosen afterward to preserve whichever companies look more appealing — otherwise this becomes exactly the kind of post-hoc selection this project has already had to correct itself on once (see `PROTOTYPE_README.md`'s v8 changelog).
3. **A pre-drawn, ranked backup list per sector**, produced at the same time as this primary list and by the same structural criteria (never chosen after seeing how a replacement performs), for the coverage check in step 2 to draw on and for handling any future acquisition/delisting prospectively: preserve historical observations for a company that gets acquired mid-run, mark it inactive as of the effective date, and bring in the next backup from that point forward — never retroactively swap it out of already-collected data.

*(The first draft of this section flagged four supposedly-still-live names — Discover Financial, Foot Locker, Catalent, Livent — as needing a delisting check. None of the four are actually in the table above; they were leftover from an earlier brainstorm list that didn't survive into the final 108, and their acquisitions have in any case already closed. No action needed on them specifically, but the general backup-list mechanism above stays, since some future company in the live 108 will eventually be acquired.)*

## What this scope buys, and an honest timeline caveat

Roughly 108 companies × ~4 earnings-related 8-Ks/year, plus ordinary-course 8-Ks (M&A, guidance updates, capacity/supply announcements), plausibly puts raw in-scope filings in the low thousands per year once extraction starts. That is not the same as an eligible candidate, and an eligible candidate is not the same as an effective, correlation-adjusted observation — a review round sharpened this into an explicit funnel worth tracking empirically rather than assuming: **raw filings → extractable events → relationship-bearing events → eligible candidates → distinct catalysts → effective N**, with real attrition at every step (an earnings 8-K with no named external party contributes a catalyst document but zero second-order candidates). Adding more companies mainly widens the top of that funnel, not the bottom — which is also why this revision resisted the temptation to go straight to 200+ companies instead of fixing what 108 already had wrong.

Two things bound the timeline, not one: enough relationship-bearing volume to reach a "low hundreds" effective sample (a company-count and funnel-yield question), and enough elapsed time to genuinely cross ≥2 volatility/rate regimes (a calendar-time question additional companies cannot substitute for — see the sector-vs-regime correction above). **A realistic expectation, if anything still slightly cautious rather than optimistic: 1–3 years to reach a promotion-gate-worthy sample at this scope**, with the first 2-3 months mainly useful for measuring where the funnel actually leaks, not for judging the final result. That is a reason to start it now rather than wait, not a reason to expect a quick answer.

## What's still not decided (the next fork)

This document only settles *which companies* the pipeline watches, and even that isn't finished (CIK verification and the coverage check are still pending). Two bigger things remain open, in priority order:

1. **The extraction runner and everything downstream of it** — the raw filing → LLM extraction → entity resolution → relationship upsert → candidate generation bridge described above. This is genuinely unbuilt, not just unconfigured, and matters more than the exact company count: no watchlist, however well-scoped, produces a single candidate signal until this exists.
2. **The infrastructure fork from earlier in this conversation** — where `edgar_ingest_worker.py` (and the new extraction runner) run continuously, where the Postgres database from `schema.sql` is hosted, what pays for the per-filing LLM extraction calls, and what monitoring exists if any of it silently stops. Still open, still needs cost/ownership decisions this document can't make on its own.
